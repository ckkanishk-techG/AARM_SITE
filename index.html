Keeping wildlife and villages safe — with sound you can't hear.

THE PROBLEM

Every year, animals wander into farms and villages — and it ends badly for someone.

Human–wildlife conflict is a growing, quiet crisis at the edge of forests everywhere. Elephants raid crops. Leopards enter cattle sheds. People are hurt trying to protect their homes, and animals are hurt — or killed — in the confrontations that follow.

Fences get broken. Firecrackers and noise only startle animals for a moment, and often push them toward another village instead of back to the forest. None of it solves the actual problem: nobody sees the animal coming, and nobody has a way to turn it back gently, before things escalate.

AARM was built to close that gap — watching the perimeter continuously, and responding the instant something approaches, in a way that neither harms nor alarms.

HOW IT WORKS

From first movement to safe morning.

01

Detect

A camera watches the perimeter around the clock.

02

Identify

AI image recognition running on a Raspberry Pi 4 identifies the animal's species.

03

Deter

AARM emits infrasound and ultrasound to turn the animal away, silent to humans.

04

Alert

IoT notifications warn people nearby in real time.

THE SILENT SPECTRUM

Working at the edges of sound.

People hear roughly 20 Hz to 20 kHz. AARM works just outside that window — frequencies most animals sense, and people never notice.

INFRASOUND
< 20 Hz
HUMAN HEARING
20 Hz – 20 kHz
ULTRASOUND
> 20 kHz
